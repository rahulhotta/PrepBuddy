## Overview

Association means that two objects know about or use one another. It does not, by itself, say who owns whom or how long either object lives.

## Key ideas

- A customer can be associated with several orders, and each order can refer to a customer.
- Associations can be one-way or two-way.
- Multiplicity describes how many objects can be connected, such as one customer to many orders.
- An association does not imply shared lifetime or ownership.
- Add only the links that the application needs; extra references make designs harder to change.

## Example

A `Doctor` and a `Patient` can be associated through `Appointment` objects. The appointment records which doctor and patient are involved and when they meet. Ending an appointment does not mean either person stops existing.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every association is ownership.
- Making every relationship bidirectional when one direction is enough.
- Forgetting to state whether the relationship is one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many.
- Keeping references that no use case needs.

## Interview angle

Describe what each side can access and how many objects can be linked. Then clarify whether the relationship represents ownership or simply a connection.
