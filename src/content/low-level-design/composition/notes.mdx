## Overview

Composition is a strong whole-and-part relationship. The whole owns or manages its parts, and those parts usually do not make sense on their own in this model.

## Key ideas

- Composition is often described as “has-a”: a document has sections.
- The whole controls creation, use, or lifetime of the parts.
- A part should not be shared between unrelated wholes if the model says it belongs to just one.
- In programming, composition can also mean building behaviour by combining smaller objects; use the context to tell which meaning is intended.
- In UML, a filled diamond marks the whole side of a composition.

## Example

A `Playlist` can own its ordered list of `PlaylistEntry` objects. Removing an entry removes it from that playlist; it does not affect another playlist. If the entries exist only as part of that playlist in the model, composition is a useful way to describe the relationship.

## Common mistakes

- Using composition for every field, even when the part has an independent lifecycle.
- Confusing UML composition with the broader programming idea of combining objects.
- Drawing a filled diamond without stating who creates and removes the parts.

## Interview angle

Explain who owns the part and what happens when the whole is deleted. Use composition when that ownership rule is important to the design.
