## Overview

Aggregation is a “has-a” relationship where the part can exist without the whole. The whole keeps a reference to the part, but does not control its entire lifetime.

## Key ideas

- Aggregation is a kind of association with a whole-and-part meaning.
- A part can be shared by, or moved between, different whole objects.
- Deleting the whole does not necessarily delete the part.
- The exact meaning can vary between modeling methods; explain the lifetime rule in plain words.
- In UML, aggregation is shown with a hollow diamond at the whole.

## Example

A `Team` has `Player` objects. A player can leave one team and join another, and still exists if the team is disbanded. The team is related to its players but does not own their entire lifetime.

## Common mistakes

- Using aggregation and composition as if they mean the same lifetime rule.
- Adding a hollow diamond without explaining what happens to parts when the whole is removed.
- Using aggregation when an ordinary association already describes the relationship clearly.

## Interview angle

Explain the part's lifetime: “The team refers to its players, but a player can exist and join another team if this team is removed.” That is more useful than relying on the word aggregation alone.
